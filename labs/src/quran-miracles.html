Counting to iron

A popular video says the Quran hides the depth of Earth’s iron core in its verse numbers. Here’s how the claim works, what holds up, and the other “miracles” people point to.

The Quran’s 114 surahs, by number of verses

وَأَنزَلْنَا الْحَدِيدَ فِيهِ بَأْسٌ شَدِيدٌ

“And We sent down iron, in which is great strength” (57:25). Surah 57 is called al-Hadid, “Iron.”

The verdict

What holds up

  • The maths: 5,075 verses come before surah 57, so 57:25 is verse 5,100.
  • The inner core does begin around 5,100–5,150 km.
  • Surah 57 is the middle surah by number.

What weakens it

  • It only works in kilometres, a unit invented in the 1790s.
  • It only works with one verse-counting tradition. Others give totals from 6,204 to 6,236.
  • With many numbers and many layers to choose from, some match is likely by chance.

Other claimed miracles

Muslims have pointed to these for centuries. Tap one to see the claim and how others read it.

Its language can’t be imitated2:23

فَأْتُوا بِسُورَةٍ مِّن مِّثْلِهِ

The claimThe Quran challenges anyone to produce one surah like it. Its rhythm, eloquence and depth are seen as beyond human authorship. This is the oldest and most central miracle in Islamic thought.
Other readingsLiterary excellence is real but hard to measure, so it convinces people differently. Critics note that every great text is unique in its own way.
The text has been preserved15:9

إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا الذِّكْرَ وَإِنَّا لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ

The claimThe text has stayed intact since revelation, kept by mass memorisation and very early manuscripts like the Birmingham fragments, carbon-dated close to the Prophet’s lifetime.
Other readingsAcademic historians largely agree the text is very old and stable, but debate details of early standardisation and the different canonical readings (qira’at).
Rome’s comeback was foretold30:2–4

غُلِبَتِ الرُّومُ ... وَهُم مِّن بَعْدِ غَلَبِهِمْ سَيَغْلِبُونَ

The claimAfter the Byzantines lost to Persia, the Quran said they would win again within a few years. They did, in campaigns around 622–627 CE.
Other readingsThe verbs can be voweled in a way that reverses who wins. A comeback was also plausible at the time, so sceptics see it as less decisive.
Pharaoh’s body kept as a sign10:92

فَالْيَوْمَ نُنَجِّيكَ بِبَدَنِكَ لِتَكُونَ لِمَنْ خَلْفَكَ آيَةً

The claimGod says Pharaoh’s body would be saved as a sign for later generations, which many link to the royal mummies found in Egypt.
Other readingsHistorians haven’t identified which pharaoh is meant, and ancient Egyptians mummified many rulers, so the link is uncertain.
Abu Lahab’s fate111:1–5

تَبَّتْ يَدَا أَبِي لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ

The claimThe surah says this uncle and enemy of the Prophet would end in ruin. He lived about ten more years and could have disproved it by simply declaring faith, but never did.
Other readingsSceptics say a committed opponent had no reason to convert just to make a point, so the outcome wasn’t surprising.
Haman at Pharaoh’s court28:38

فَأَوْقِدْ لِي يَا هَامَانُ عَلَى الطِّينِ

The claimPharaoh asks Haman to bake bricks and build a tower. Some writers argue an Egyptian official with a similar name appears in old inscriptions.
Other readingsMost Egyptologists don’t accept that reading. Haman is better known from the Book of Esther, set centuries later in Persia.
“King” for Joseph, “Pharaoh” for Moses12:43

وَقَالَ الْمَلِكُ إِنِّي أَرَىٰ سَبْعَ بَقَرَاتٍ

The claimJoseph’s ruler is called malik (king), Moses’s is Fir‘awn. Egyptians only began calling their kings “pharaoh” later, so the Quran’s wording fits history.
Other readingsNeither Joseph’s nor Moses’s era can be dated with confidence, so it’s hard to test whether the titles fit.
The splitting of the moon54:1

اقْتَرَبَتِ السَّاعَةُ وَانشَقَّ الْقَمَرُ

The claimTraditional reports describe the moon splitting as a sign in Mecca. Online posts say NASA photos show a crack across it.
Other readingsThe feature in those photos is an ordinary lunar groove, and NASA scientists have said there’s no evidence of a split. Some commentators read the verse as about the end of time.
Returning safely to Mecca48:27

لَتَدْخُلُنَّ الْمَسْجِدَ الْحَرَامَ إِن شَاءَ اللَّهُ آمِنِينَ

The claimAfter Muslims were turned away at Hudaybiyyah in 628, this verse promised they would enter the Sacred Mosque in safety. They did the following year.
Other readingsThe treaty signed at Hudaybiyyah already allowed a pilgrimage the next year, so the outcome was expected.
Iram of the pillars89:6–8

أَلَمْ تَرَ كَيْفَ فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ بِعَادٍ إِرَمَ ذَاتِ الْعِمَادِ

The claimThe Quran names Iram, a city of lofty pillars destroyed with the people of ‘Ad. A site found in Oman’s desert in the 1990s, with towers and a sinkhole collapse, is said to be it.
Other readingsScholars don’t generally accept that the site is Iram. It’s small for a famous city, and Iram may have been a tribe or region.
Thamud’s rock-carved homes89:9

وَثَمُودَ الَّذِينَ جَابُوا الصَّخْرَ بِالْوَادِ

The claimThamud carved dwellings from rock, and archaeology has confirmed the people and the carved sites in northwest Arabia.
Other readingsThamud appear in Assyrian and Greek records, and the famous tombs at Hegra are Nabataean. Arabs of the time already knew these places.
The flood of Sheba’s dam34:15–16

فَأَرْسَلْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ سَيْلَ الْعَرِمِ

The claimSheba’s gardens were ruined by a great flood, matching the collapse of the Marib dam in Yemen.
Other readingsThe dam’s final collapse happened around 570 CE, so it was recent and well-known news in Arabia.
Noah’s ark on Judi11:44

وَاسْتَوَتْ عَلَى الْجُودِيِّ

The claimUnlike the Bible’s Ararat, the Quran names Mount Judi, and some argue this location fits better.
Other readingsSyriac Christian and Jewish traditions already placed the ark in the same Qardu mountains before Islam.
Bakka, the old name of Mecca3:96

إِنَّ أَوَّلَ بَيْتٍ وُضِعَ لِلنَّاسِ لَلَّذِي بِبَكَّةَ

The claimThe Quran calls Mecca “Bakka,” and some link it to the “valley of Baca” in Psalm 84, suggesting the Bible mentions Mecca.
Other readingsMost biblical scholars read Baca as a “valley of weeping” or of balsam trees on the road to Jerusalem.
The crow that teaches burial5:31

فَبَعَثَ اللَّهُ غُرَابًا يَبْحَثُ فِي الْأَرْضِ

The claimA crow shows Adam’s son how to bury his brother, and modern research shows crows are among the most intelligent birds.
Other readingsA similar story, with a bird showing Adam and Eve how to bury Abel, appears in earlier Jewish tradition.

Modern “scientific” claims

These became popular in the 20th century. Most turn on how a single Arabic word is translated.

Stages of the embryo23:12–14

فَكَسَوْنَا الْعِظَامَ لَحْمًا

The claimThe verses describe stages, from a drop to a clinging thing to a chewed-like lump, then bones clothed with flesh, before anyone could see an embryo.
Other readingsCritics say this matches the Greek physician Galen’s model from the 2nd century, including the bones-then-flesh order, which biology doesn’t support.
An expanding universe51:47

وَالسَّمَاءَ بَنَيْنَاهَا بِأَيْدٍ وَإِنَّا لَمُوسِعُونَ

The claim“We are expanding it” is read as describing the expansion of the universe, discovered in the 1920s.
Other readingsThe word mūsi‘ūn can also mean “We have ample power.” Many older commentators read it that way.
A barrier between two seas55:19–20

مَرَجَ الْبَحْرَيْنِ يَلْتَقِيَانِ بَيْنَهُمَا بَرْزَخٌ لَّا يَبْغِيَانِ

The claimThis describes the zones where seas of different saltiness meet without fully mixing, as oceanographers have observed.
Other readingsClassical commentators read it as fresh and salt water meeting, something people could already see at river mouths.
Mountains as pegs78:7

وَالْجِبَالَ أَوْتَادًا

The claimMountains have deep roots below them, like tent pegs, which geology confirmed much later.
Other readingsMountains do have crustal roots, but they don’t hold the Earth steady the way the claim often implies.
Fingertips75:4

بَلَىٰ قَادِرِينَ عَلَىٰ أَن نُّسَوِّيَ بَنَانَهُ

The claimGod can restore even a person’s fingertips, read as a hint at unique fingerprints.
Other readingsThe verse is about resurrection down to the finest detail and doesn’t mention uniqueness.
The female bee16:68–69

أَنِ اتَّخِذِي مِنَ الْجِبَالِ بُيُوتًا

The claimThe verbs telling the bee to build and eat are feminine, and worker bees are female.
Other readingsArabic often uses feminine forms for collective nouns like “bees,” so the grammar may not point to biology.
Iron sent down57:25

وَأَنزَلْنَا الْحَدِيدَ

The claimIron is made in stars and arrives in meteorites, so “sent down” fits its cosmic origin.
Other readingsThe same verb is used for livestock (39:6) and clothing (7:26), so it often means “bestowed.”
The Big Bang21:30

أَنَّ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ كَانَتَا رَتْقًا فَفَتَقْنَاهُمَا

The claimThe heavens and earth were “joined, then We split them apart,” read as everything starting from one point.
Other readingsClassical commentators also read it as the sky opening with rain and the earth opening with plants. Ancient creation stories often describe sky and earth being separated.
The universe began as smoke41:11

ثُمَّ اسْتَوَىٰ إِلَى السَّمَاءِ وَهِيَ دُخَانٌ

The claimThe sky was once “smoke,” matching the gas and dust clouds that stars and planets formed from.
Other readingsThe verse comes after the earth is created (41:9–10), while in cosmology the earth formed much later than the first gas clouds.
Every living thing from water21:30

وَجَعَلْنَا مِنَ الْمَاءِ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ حَيٍّ

The claimAll life depends on water and cells are mostly water, long before microscopes.
Other readingsTrue for life on Earth, but water as the source of life was already a common ancient idea, for example in Greek philosophy.
Sun and moon in orbits21:33

كُلٌّ فِي فَلَكٍ يَسْبَحُونَ

The claimEach “swims in an orbit,” including the sun, which we now know moves around the galaxy.
Other readingsThe verse can also describe their daily path across the sky, which everyone could already see.
Mountains passing like clouds27:88

وَتَرَى الْجِبَالَ تَحْسَبُهَا جَامِدَةً وَهِيَ تَمُرُّ مَرَّ السَّحَابِ

The claimMountains look still but are moving, read as plate tectonics or Earth’s rotation.
Other readingsThe surrounding verses are about the Day of Judgment, so many commentators read it as describing the end of the world.
Darkness and waves in the deep sea24:40

أَوْ كَظُلُمَاتٍ فِي بَحْرٍ لُّجِّيٍّ يَغْشَاهُ مَوْجٌ مِّن فَوْقِهِ مَوْجٌ

The claimLayers of darkness and “waves above waves” match deep-sea darkness and internal waves under the surface.
Other readingsThe verse is a parable about disbelief. Critics see vivid imagery of a stormy night at sea rather than oceanography.
Rain clouds and hail24:43

وَيُنَزِّلُ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مِن جِبَالٍ فِيهَا مِن بَرَدٍ

The claimClouds are gathered, stacked into “mountains,” and send down hail, just like towering storm clouds.
Other readingsTall storm clouds and hail are visible from the ground, so careful observers could describe them.
The lowest land30:3

فِي أَدْنَى الْأَرْضِ

The claimThe Romans were defeated in adna al-ard, read as “the lowest land,” and the Dead Sea area is the lowest land on Earth.
Other readingsClassical scholars and most translations read adna as “nearest.” Historians also don’t place the key battles at the Dead Sea.
Pain felt through the skin4:56

كُلَّمَا نَضِجَتْ جُلُودُهُم بَدَّلْنَاهُمْ جُلُودًا غَيْرَهَا لِيَذُوقُوا الْعَذَابَ

The claimBurnt skins are replaced so pain is felt again, matching how deep burns destroy pain nerves.
Other readingsPeople already knew burns hurt through the skin. The verse describes punishment, not anatomy.
The lying forelock96:15–16

نَاصِيَةٍ كَاذِبَةٍ خَاطِئَةٍ

The claimThe front of the head is called lying and sinful, matching the frontal lobe’s role in decisions.
Other readingsIn Arabic, holding someone by the forelock means having full control of them, and nasiyah often stands for the whole person.
Tight chest at altitude6:125

كَأَنَّمَا يَصَّعَّدُ فِي السَّمَاءِ

The claimA chest feels tight “as if climbing into the sky,” matching thinner air at height.
Other readingsThe image works as a metaphor for a closed heart, and people who climbed mountains already knew the feeling.
Sex decided by the sperm53:45–46

وَأَنَّهُ خَلَقَ الزَّوْجَيْنِ الذَّكَرَ وَالْأُنثَىٰ مِن نُّطْفَةٍ إِذَا تُمْنَىٰ

The claimMale and female come from an emitted drop, and sperm does determine a baby’s sex.
Other readingsThe verse doesn’t contrast the father with the mother, so it may just describe conception in general.
The female spider and ant29:41, 27:18

كَمَثَلِ الْعَنكَبُوتِ اتَّخَذَتْ بَيْتًا

The claimThe spider that builds a house is grammatically female, as are web-building spiders. The speaking ant is female too, like worker ants.
Other readingsAs with the bee, Arabic often treats these words as feminine anyway, so the grammar may not point to biology.
Hearing before sight16:78

وَجَعَلَ لَكُمُ السَّمْعَ وَالْأَبْصَارَ وَالْأَفْئِدَةَ

The claimThe Quran nearly always lists hearing before sight, and a baby in the womb can hear before its eyes respond to light.
Other readings“Hearing and sight” is a natural word order in Arabic, like “ears and eyes.” The order may be style rather than biology.
Milk from between gut and blood16:66

مِن بَيْنِ فَرْثٍ وَدَمٍ لَّبَنًا خَالِصًا

The claimPure milk comes “from between digested food and blood,” matching how nutrients pass from the gut to the blood to the udder.
Other readingsGreek thinkers like Aristotle already taught that milk is made from blood, so the idea wasn’t new.
What the fly takes22:73

وَإِن يَسْلُبْهُمُ الذُّبَابُ شَيْئًا لَّا يَسْتَنقِذُوهُ مِنْهُ

The claimIf a fly snatches something, it can’t be taken back, because flies start digesting food outside their bodies.
Other readingsMany creatures, like spiders, digest food externally. The verse’s point is that idols are too weak to stop even a fly.
An egg-shaped Earth79:30

وَالْأَرْضَ بَعْدَ ذَٰلِكَ دَحَاهَا

The claimDahaha is linked to udhiyya, an ostrich’s nest or egg, so the verse describes Earth’s slightly squashed shape.
Other readingsClassical dictionaries and translations give “spread it out.” Earth is also flattened at the poles, not egg-shaped.
A round Earth39:5

يُكَوِّرُ اللَّيْلَ عَلَى النَّهَارِ

The claimNight is “wrapped” over day like a turban is wound, which only works on a round Earth.
Other readingsGreek scholars knew Earth was round and even measured its size about 800 years earlier.
The moon’s borrowed light71:16

وَجَعَلَ الْقَمَرَ فِيهِنَّ نُورًا وَجَعَلَ الشَّمْسَ سِرَاجًا

The claimThe sun is called a lamp and the moon a light, a distinction that fits the moon reflecting sunlight.
Other readingsGreek astronomers knew the moon reflects sunlight centuries earlier, and nur doesn’t specifically mean reflected light.
The sun’s resting place36:38

وَالشَّمْسُ تَجْرِي لِمُسْتَقَرٍّ لَّهَا

The claimThe sun runs toward a fixed point or end, matching its motion through space and its limited lifespan.
Other readingsA well-known hadith links this to the sun prostrating beneath God’s throne each night, suggesting its daily path across the sky.
A protected roof21:32

وَجَعَلْنَا السَّمَاءَ سَقْفًا مَّحْفُوظًا

The claimThe sky is a “protected roof,” read as the atmosphere and magnetic field shielding us from radiation and meteors.
Other readingsThe sky as a canopy over the earth is a common ancient image, and other verses say it is protected from devils.
The sky that returns86:11

وَالسَّمَاءِ ذَاتِ الرَّجْعِ

The claimThe sky “returns” things: rain to the earth, and radio waves and heat back down from the atmosphere.
Other readingsClassical commentators read it simply as rain returning again and again, which everyone could see.
Fertilising winds15:22

وَأَرْسَلْنَا الرِّيَاحَ لَوَاقِحَ

The claimWinds are sent “fertilising,” matching how they carry pollen and help clouds form rain.
Other readingsArabs hand-pollinated date palms and knew plants needed pollen. The verse goes on to rain, a visible effect of wind.
Pairs in everything51:49

وَمِن كُلِّ شَيْءٍ خَلَقْنَا زَوْجَيْنِ

The claimEverything is created in pairs, matching male and female plants, positive and negative charges, and matter and antimatter.
Other readingsThe statement is broad enough to fit almost any pairing, from day and night to heaven and earth.
Smaller than an atom10:61

مِن مِّثْقَالِ ذَرَّةٍ ... وَلَا أَصْغَرَ مِن ذَٰلِكَ

The claimGod knows the weight of a dharrah “or smaller,” hinting that atoms can be split.
Other readingsDharrah originally meant a tiny ant or a speck of dust. Modern Arabic borrowed it for “atom.”
Seven earths65:12

خَلَقَ سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ وَمِنَ الْأَرْضِ مِثْلَهُنَّ

The claimGod made seven heavens “and of the earth, the like of them,” read as Earth’s layers.
Other readingsGeologists divide Earth into different numbers of layers depending on the model, and many commentators read it as seven separate earths.
Healing in honey16:69

فِيهِ شِفَاءٌ لِّلنَّاسِ

The claimHoney has healing in it, and modern research confirms it fights bacteria and helps wounds heal.
Other readingsAncient Egyptians and Greeks already used honey as medicine, so its value was well known.
Black holes81:15–16

فَلَا أُقْسِمُ بِالْخُنَّسِ الْجَوَارِ الْكُنَّسِ

The claimAn oath by hidden, moving things that “sweep” is read as black holes, which are invisible and pull in everything nearby.
Other readingsClassical commentators read it as planets that seem to move backward and vanish, or stars hidden by daylight.
The knocking star86:1–3

وَالسَّمَاءِ وَالطَّارِقِ ... النَّجْمُ الثَّاقِبُ

The claimTariq comes from a root meaning to knock, matching pulsars, stars that send out regular pulses.
Other readingsTariq usually means a night visitor, and the verse itself explains it as a star of piercing brightness.
The falling places of stars56:75

فَلَا أُقْسِمُ بِمَوَاقِعِ النُّجُومِ

The claimGod swears by the “positions” of stars, which fits the fact that we see stars where they were long ago, since their light takes years to reach us.
Other readingsMawaqi‘ commonly means where stars set. Some classical scholars read it as the stages of revelation.
Time and the speed of light32:5

فِي يَوْمٍ كَانَ مِقْدَارُهُ أَلْفَ سَنَةٍ مِّمَّا تَعُدُّونَ

The claimA day “like a thousand years” shows time is relative. Some go further and calculate the speed of light from the moon’s orbit.
Other readingsThe calculation needs several chosen adjustments, and the ratio drifts as Earth’s day and the moon’s orbit change. Another verse (70:4) says 50,000 years.
Fire beneath the sea52:6

وَالْبَحْرِ الْمَسْجُورِ

The claimAn oath by the “blazing sea” fits volcanoes and hot vents on the ocean floor.
Other readingsMasjur can also mean “filled” or “held back,” and a related verse (81:6) describes seas burning at the end of time.
The earth that splits86:12

وَالْأَرْضِ ذَاتِ الصَّدْعِ

The claimThe earth “which cracks open” is read as the cracks between tectonic plates.
Other readingsIt sits next to “the sky that returns,” and commentators read it as soil splitting open as plants sprout after rain.
Travel beyond the sky55:33

إِنِ اسْتَطَعْتُمْ أَن تَنفُذُوا مِنْ أَقْطَارِ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ فَانفُذُوا ۚ لَا تَنفُذُونَ إِلَّا بِسُلْطَانٍ

The claimHumans and jinn can pass beyond the heavens only with sultan, read as power or technology, foretelling space travel.
Other readingsThe next verses describe flames sent against anyone trying to escape, so most commentators read it as a challenge on Judgment Day.
Three darknesses in the womb39:6

يَخْلُقُكُمْ فِي بُطُونِ أُمَّهَاتِكُمْ خَلْقًا مِّن بَعْدِ خَلْقٍ فِي ظُلُمَاتٍ ثَلَاثٍ

The claimThe baby grows in “three darknesses,” matching the three layers around it: the belly wall, the womb and the membranes.
Other readingsEarly commentators already explained it as belly, womb and placenta, which people knew from childbirth and animals.
Seven layered heavens67:3

الَّذِي خَلَقَ سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ طِبَاقًا

The claimSeven heavens in layers match the layers of Earth’s atmosphere.
Other readingsScientists usually list five main layers, and another verse (67:5) puts the stars in the lowest heaven, which doesn’t fit the atmosphere.
Pillars you cannot see13:2

اللَّهُ الَّذِي رَفَعَ السَّمَاوَاتِ بِغَيْرِ عَمَدٍ تَرَوْنَهَا

The claimThe heavens are raised “without pillars that you see,” hinting at invisible pillars like gravity.
Other readingsThe Arabic can also mean “without pillars, as you can see,” and classical scholars were split between the two readings.
The mosquito and what’s on it2:26

أَن يَضْرِبَ مَثَلًا مَّا بَعُوضَةً فَمَا فَوْقَهَا

The claimGod gives the example of a mosquito “and what is above it,” read as tiny mites that ride on mosquitoes.
Other readingsClassical commentators read “above it” as anything bigger, or anything even smaller. The point is that no example is too humble.
Rain that feeds springs39:21

أَلَمْ تَرَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ أَنزَلَ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً فَسَلَكَهُ يَنَابِيعَ فِي الْأَرْضِ

The claimRain sinks into the ground and comes out as springs, describing the water cycle when many thinkers believed springs came from the sea.
Other readingsThe Roman writer Vitruvius had already explained that springs are fed by rain, centuries earlier.
Heavy clouds and lightning13:12

هُوَ الَّذِي يُرِيكُمُ الْبَرْقَ خَوْفًا وَطَمَعًا وَيُنشِئُ السَّحَابَ الثِّقَالَ

The claimClouds are called “heavy,” and we now know a single storm cloud can hold hundreds of thousands of tonnes of water.
Other readingsDark, rain-filled clouds look heavy, and “heavy clouds” is a natural description anyone could use.
The sun folded up81:1

إِذَا الشَّمْسُ كُوِّرَتْ

The claimThe sun will be “wound up” and its light will end, matching the fact that stars have a limited life.
Other readingsThe verse describes the Day of Judgment, and the idea of the heavens ending is common to many religions.

Number patterns

These claims count words or verses. The result always depends on what you choose to count.

The middle nation in the middle verse2:143

وَكَذَٰلِكَ جَعَلْنَاكُمْ أُمَّةً وَسَطًا

The claimAl-Baqarah has 286 verses, and the verse calling Muslims a “middle nation” is number 143, exactly half.
Other readingsIt only works with the Kufan count. Other classical counts give al-Baqarah a slightly different number of verses.
365 days, 12 monthsword counts
The claimThe singular word for “day” appears 365 times and “month” 12 times.
Other readingsCounts change with the rules. Forms of “day” appear over 100 more times, and “month” appears about 21 times in all forms, around 9 in the singular.
Land and sea in Earth’s ratioword counts
The claim“Sea” appears 32 times and “land” 13 times. That’s 71% and 29%, matching Earth’s surface.
Other readingsThe result depends on which Arabic words count as “land.” The real ratio also shifts with ice ages and sea levels.
Matching word pairsword counts
The claimOpposites appear equally often, like life and death (145 each), or angels and devils (88 each).
Other readingsIndependent counts often disagree, and with thousands of possible word pairs, some will match by chance.
The code of 1974:30

عَلَيْهَا تِسْعَةَ عَشَرَ

The claim“Over it are nineteen.” Rashad Khalifa argued in the 1970s that letters and words are built on multiples of 19. The site used in the iron video comes from his movement.
Other readingsHis system required removing two verses (9:128–129), which mainstream Muslims reject. Most scholars don’t accept the code.
300 years, and add nine18:25

وَلَبِثُوا فِي كَهْفِهِمْ ثَلَاثَ مِائَةٍ سِنِينَ وَازْدَادُوا تِسْعًا

The claimThe sleepers of the cave stayed “300 years, and add nine.” 300 solar years is almost exactly 309 lunar years, so the verse gives both calendars at once.
Other readingsThe maths is right, but the calendar gap was well known before Islam. Some scholars reject this reading and take it as simply 309 years.
Jesus and Adam, 25 times each3:59

إِنَّ مَثَلَ عِيسَىٰ عِندَ اللَّهِ كَمَثَلِ آدَمَ

The claimThe Quran says Jesus is like Adam, and both names appear exactly 25 times.
Other readingsThe match depends on what you count, such as whether “children of Adam” or “the Messiah” are included.
Mecca at the golden ratio6:92

وَلِتُنذِرَ أُمَّ الْقُرَىٰ وَمَنْ حَوْلَهَا

The claimMecca, “the mother of cities,” sits at the golden ratio between the North and South Poles.
Other readingsEvery place at that latitude does too, and the east-west version needs a chosen map edge. The Quran itself doesn’t make this claim.

How scholars see it

The traditional miracle of the Quran is its language and message. The scientific readings are newer and divide Muslims themselves. Writers like Maurice Bucaille and Zaghloul el-Naggar popularised them, while many scholars warn against tying scripture to scientific theories that can change.

Verse counts follow the Hafs (Kufan) count of 6,236 verses. Earth depths are standard textbook values.
